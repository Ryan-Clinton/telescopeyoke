A SynScan mount that centres its own GoTos

TelescopeYoke drives an ordinary Sky-Watcher SynScan mount through its handset, and measures what a careful setup would otherwise have to get right by hand.

The handset is the proven route. Without a handset, through Sky-Watcher's Wi-Fi adapter, it has found home and made a GoTo on the same mount (16 and 19 November 2026), but not yet run a night; an EQDIR lead is untried.

GoTo pointing errors, corrected by plate solving

A GoTo from a home position set by eye lands off target. TelescopeYoke photographs the sky, plate-solves where the telescope really ended up, corrects and repeats. On its first real night an EQ3 that started about ten degrees out centred M27 in three corrections: 107′ off, then 6′, then 1′.

Home found by measurement, not by the marks

After a polar alignment it measures where home really is, from plate solves either side of the meridian, drives there and has you mark the joints. Proven under real stars on 17 October 2026: an EQ3 set 10.66° out by eye, found to 0.19°.

And the rest of a night

  • Polar alignment by plate solving, saying which bolt to turn and which way (Calibrating a mount).
  • Focusing with a motor focuser, or by ear with a manual one (Focus).
  • Imaging runs, stacked as they go, with re-centring between blocks and the cloud noticed (Imaging). The night's end by itself, parked and the person told, is built but not yet proven through a real night.
  • Limits kept in the code: never below 20° altitude, the meridian limit, 40° from the Sun; every move is checked against them before it is sent, and --dry-run shows a move without making it.
