# The camera's row stripes: what others have found

Researched 19 October 2026, for the green and magenta row stripes of the Altair Hypercam 183C (`docs/validation.md`, `stacking.destripe`). Other people have seen them. Horizontal row banding on Altair Hypercam 183C and 183M cameras is widely reported. One owner of the same fan-cooled 183C says it starts **below about 10 °C and gets worse as it gets colder**, and that **wrapping a dew heater round the camera body stopped it** ([Altair Astro Facebook group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/8796706147100995/)). Other Altair 183 owners give the same picture: banding below unity gain, and banding cured by changing the USB port, laptop, lead or hub, or by fastening a loose USB plug. SharpCap's author suspects the cause is ripple on the USB supply voltage while the sensor is read out, though he says he has never proved it ([SharpCap forum](https://forums.sharpcap.co.uk/viewtopic.php?t=5068)). Our pattern fits that power-and-readout explanation better than a sensor defect. The bands are new in every frame, worse where signal is small, unaffected by offset and fan, worst at −1 °C in damp air, and almost gone indoors. Long exposures failing on the coldest night point the same way, at a marginal USB power or connection path, or a cold-sensitive fault in the camera. Neither Altair nor Touptek has published any firmware or SDK fix for IMX183 banding. Do not flash firmware blind. Processing can only level the random part, which stacking.destripe already does in the right way. So the next steps are physical. Keep the camera warm. Give it a short, firm, separately powered USB path. Shoot at or above unity gain with longer subs. Then ask in the Altair Facebook groups and on indi-3rdparty with the test results attached.

## Altair 183 owners report the same bands, and some of their cures worked

The closest match comes from a Hypercam 183C thread in the Altair Astro Facebook group, about 2025. One owner wrote: "I've got the 183C fan cooled and also get banding ... it occurs when the temperature drops below 10 degrees. Worse as the drops lower." He kept the camera above 10 °C with a dew heater wrapped round it, and reported "Seems to work!" ([Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/8796706147100995/)). Nobody tested his claim independently. Even so, it is the only report anywhere that ties the banding of this exact camera to ambient temperature, and it matches ours being nearly gone indoors and warm. In the same thread, a long-time Altair 183C owner says "do not use less than Unity Gain as this causes banding. Gain 400 appears to be the sweet spot". The same owner makes the point in the 183 users' group: "Any gain lower then unity (400) and I get horizontal lines" ([FB 183 users group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/465061658206316/posts/1107541107291698/)). The low-gain effect is not unique to Altair. QHY183 owners say "too low a gain will result in too much banding" ([Cloudy Nights](https://www.cloudynights.com/forums/topic/772536-is-there-an-ideal-gain-for-the-qhy183mm-camera/)).

Electrical cures are the next most common reports, and several are first-hand. An Altair 183MC had horizontal noise bands on long exposures. A ferrite and gain changes did nothing, but the owner noticed "a slight bit of play in the USB port so I fastened the cable securely to the camera body ... bingo, no more horizontal noise bands" ([FB 183 users group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/465061658206316/posts/1107541107291698/)). Under another Hypercam 183C post where lines appeared suddenly, the replies are mostly first-hand. One owner cleared the bands by separating a power cable from the USB lead. Another fixed them with a powered USB hub because worn laptop ports were the cause. A third had a faulty lead. Another wrote "Had the same camera and issue. It was a USB issue. Sorted by using a powered USB hub ... Not all laptops/PCs put out enough amps/voltage" ([Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/6673441896094108/)). A Hypercam 183C Tec owner said the problem was "seemingly going now that I've been using an upowered USB2 hub" ([Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/permalink/1946139525491059/)). Another 183C owner found that one laptop USB port gave more banding than the other, and that interference on the cables also caused it ([Cloudy Nights](https://www.cloudynights.com/forums/topic/822496-ngc-4725-challenge-techniques-and-progress-discussion/page/2/)). A QHY183M owner traced bands that "moved between images" to the USB hub in his Pegasus power box, and moving to a separate hub cured them ([SGP forum](https://forum.sequencegeneratorpro.com/t/qhy183m-is-this-normal-or-do-i-have-a-problem/9633)).

Two other outcomes are worth knowing. An Altair 26C owner had banding outdoors that could not be reproduced indoors, and Altair's tests found the camera "working perfectly". That is the same indoor/outdoor split we see, and it points at the outdoor electrical environment rather than the sensor ([SharpCap forum](https://forums.sharpcap.co.uk/viewtopic.php?p=34183)). At the other extreme, a 183C ProTec owner's banding "just got worse" whatever he changed, and "signalled the death of my sensor" ([Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/8796706147100995/)). ZWO told a used ASI183MM Pro owner to send the camera for repair after cables, power supply, settings and laptop had all been swapped without effect ([ZWO forum](https://bbs.zwoastro.com/d/12355-asi183mm-pro-horizontal-lines)). So a failing camera is possible. It should be considered only after the cheap physical tests fail, and especially if the bands get worse over the coming weeks.

## Supply noise during readout explains every one of our symptoms

In the engineering literature, random row noise comes from noise on the sensor's supply or ADC reference voltages. Such noise shifts a whole row's offset at once during readout, so the pattern is new in every frame ([arXiv 2012.03666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03666); [US7508430B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7508430)). Robin Glover reaches the same view from practice. He suspects "fluctuations in the power supply voltage (USB) when the image is being read from the sensor", and says that different PCs regulate USB voltage better or worse ([SharpCap forum](https://forums.sharpcap.co.uk/viewtopic.php?t=5068)). The Hypercam 183C has **no external supply at all**. Its sensor, FPGA, 4 GB DDR and fan all run from the USB cable ([Astroworld listing](https://astroworldtelescopes.com/products/altair-hypercam-183c-color-cmos-camera-w-4-gb-ddr-ram); [Altair fan vs TEC FAQ](https://www.altairastro.help/info-instructions/faq/what-are-the-advantages-or-disadvantages-of-altair-hypercam-fan-cooled-cameras-vs-tec/)). Every millivolt of ripple or drop on that one lead reaches the readout electronics.

Each of our observations follows from this. The offset is added per readout, so it is new in every frame. That is why dithering gained nothing measurable over 18 minutes. Dithering only spreads out patterns that are fixed to sensor rows ([Stargazers Lounge](https://stargazerslounge.com/profile/46703-wimvb/content/page/32)), and a random pattern only falls as the square root of the number of frames. The amount per readout is roughly fixed, so it looms largest against little signal: low gain and short exposures. Offset 0 versus 50 made no difference, which rules out clipping at zero, the explanation another Touptek camera's owner was given ([Cloudy Nights](https://cloudynights.com/topic/853896-risingcam-severe-banding-issues-and-their-support)). Fan on or off made no difference, which removes the fan as the noise source. The green/magenta colour needs no special mechanism. If the R/G rows and G/B rows of the RGGB mosaic take slightly different offsets, or the offset changes along a row, debayering turns the difference into colour. Row-noise patents note that a correction treating all colours alike "is wrong for CFA sensors" ([US7508430B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7508430)). This is why stacking.destripe's levelling per Bayer site, in sections across the frame, is the right shape of correction.

The cold is the open question, and here the explanation becomes inference. No source measures IMX183 row noise against temperature. Two cold mechanisms fit what happens. The first is a marginal contact (a worn USB socket, a loose plug, a cracked joint) that changes resistance as metal contracts. The second is damp air at −1 °C condensing on the board or connector of a fan-cooled body that draws outside air through it. Both would also explain long exposures failing to arrive on the same night. One Altair 183C Pro owner on INDI described timeouts that cleared after 10 minutes to over an hour, "as if it needs to warm up", and Altair later replaced a faulty camera in that same thread ([INDI forum, Altair 269c](https://www.indilib.org/forum/ccds-dslrs/10249-altair-269c.html)). The laptop being on mains rules out battery voltage sag. It does not rule out ripple from the mains charger, or a ground loop between the charger and the mount's supply. One 183C owner suspected exactly that, and found "another laptop ... was much better" ([Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/3188080564630276/)).

## Firmware, SDK and settings offer levers but no published fix

No firmware list, changelog or download for the Hypercam 183C exists in public, and no vendor statement names IMX183 row banding. The Touptek SDK can flash firmware from Linux (`Toupcam_Update` with a `.ufw` file). Its own header warns that a power loss during the update leaves the camera able only to "be returned to the factory for repair" ([toupcam.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/indilib/indi-3rdparty/master/libtoupcam/toupcam.h)). Touptek's rule is that firmware works only with an SDK newer than itself. INDI users who flashed newer firmware were left with cameras "completely defunct or... partially functional" ([indi-3rdparty #1114](https://github.com/indilib/indi-3rdparty/issues/1114)). **Firmware 1.4.1 is not a known cause, and flashing anything without Altair's own file and instructions risks the camera for no evidenced gain.**

Fixes for Touptek-family camera faults have come from SDK versions, not firmware, and they have gone both ways. An Altair 26C failed exactly at 60 s exposures until a newer SDK fixed it ([NINA issue #711](https://bitbucket.org/Isbeorn/nina/issues/711/unable-to-take-60s-exposures-with-altair)). A 24CFX owner reports that the December 2024 Altair driver gave "very severe banding", and that going back to the previous `altaircam.dll` cured it completely. He adds that nobody else has confirmed this ([Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/8796706147100995/)). Our SDK, 60.31589, is older than the 60.32226 (August 2026) now bundled in INDI ([altaircam.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/indilib/indi-3rdparty/master/libaltaircam/altaircam.h)). One INDI user found 57.27348 the last SDK with no timeouts at all ([#1114](https://github.com/indilib/indi-3rdparty/issues/1114)). Trying both costs nothing. We saw the long-exposure failure under the raw SDK too, so INDI's own timeout logic is not the cause.

The SDK has no row-noise option. The settings that change the pattern are the speed level and USB bandwidth. Altair's own staff say changing the USB speed "can eliminate banding of some frequencies" ([Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/9050064195098521/)). A 24CFX owner found "a sweet spot" on the 0–9 scale ([Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/8796706147100995/)). QHY says changing USB traffic "changes the sensor driver frequency" ([IceInSpace](https://www.iceinspace.com.au/forum/showthread.php?p=1031937)). Low-noise mode and HCG would help if the 2017 non-Pro model supports them. The INDI driver shows those switches only when the camera reports the matching flags ([indi_toupbase.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/indilib/indi-3rdparty/master/indi-toupbase/indi_toupbase.cpp)). Altair's own explanation for dropped frames is host power saving: USB power cut-off and CPU idle states ([altairastro.help](https://www.altairastro.help/info-instructions/troubleshooting/interrupted-frames-subs-download-during-imaging-session/)). On Linux the equivalents are USB autosuspend and CPU C-states, which are worth ruling out for the >2 s failures.

## Try these in order, cheapest and most decisive first

The order puts first the tests that separate the camera's temperature from its electrical surroundings, because those decide everything after them. Darks are the measuring tool throughout. They need no sky, and they show banding without stars confusing the row estimates.

| # | Step | What it decides | Basis |
|---|------|-----------------|-------|
| 1 | Take a set of short darks at low gain with the camera cold and the laptop indoors (camera outside on the same lead, or in a fridge in a sealed bag with desiccant). Then take the same set with the camera warm and the rest of the rig outdoors. | Whether the camera's temperature or the outdoor electrical environment causes the bands | Inference, built on the [Altair 26C indoor/outdoor case](https://forums.sharpcap.co.uk/viewtopic.php?p=34183) and [the below-10 °C report](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/8796706147100995/) |
| 2 | Wrap a dew strap round the camera body, powered from its own battery, not the mount supply. This also keeps damp off. | Whether keeping the camera above about 10 °C removes the bands and the long-exposure failures | [Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/8796706147100995/) |
| 3 | Use the short Altair-supplied lead into the laptop's other working port. Tie the plug firmly to the camera body. Route the lead away from the mount's power cable. | Whether a loose contact or induced pick-up causes the bands | [183 users group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/465061658206316/posts/1107541107291698/); [Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/6673441896094108/) |
| 4 | Add a powered USB hub with its own supply. Try USB2 as well as USB3. | Whether a weak or noisy laptop USB rail causes the bands | [Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/6673441896094108/); [Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/permalink/1946139525491059/); [SGP QHY183M](https://forum.sequencegeneratorpro.com/t/qhy183m-is-this-normal-or-do-i-have-a-problem/9633) |
| 5 | Step through the speed levels and look for one where the bands weaken. | Whether a beat between the readout clock and the supply noise sets the pattern | [Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/9050064195098521/) |
| 6 | Image at unity gain or above, with subs long enough that sky noise clearly exceeds the row noise. | How much of the problem the existing pipeline can remove | [183 users group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/465061658206316/posts/1107541107291698/); [AstroBin swamp rule](https://app.astrobin.com/forum/topic/144256/narrowband-exposure-lengths-vs-swamping-read-noise-help) |
| 7 | Turn off USB autosuspend for the camera and try limiting CPU idle states. Then try SDK 60.32226, and 57.27348 if needed. Capture RAW, not RGB. | Whether the >2 s failures come from host power saving or the SDK | [Altair help](https://www.altairastro.help/info-instructions/troubleshooting/interrupted-frames-subs-download-during-imaging-session/); [#1114](https://github.com/indilib/indi-3rdparty/issues/1114); [#1039](https://github.com/indilib/indi-3rdparty/issues/1039) |

Steps 1 and 2 can run on the same cold evening, and they show where the trouble starts. If the camera bands when cold even with the laptop indoors, and the dew strap cures it, the fix is a permanent heater. If the bands follow the outdoor rig whatever the camera's temperature, steps 3 and 4 are where the cure lies. If nothing changes the bands and they keep getting worse, a diagnosis from Altair, and possibly a repair, is the honest answer.

Processing has little left to give. Siril's `seqfixbanding` and SharpCap's Banding Suppression both level whole rows from row averages, the same idea as stacking.destripe but less refined ([Siril docs](https://siril.readthedocs.io/en/latest/processing/banding.html); [SharpCap forum](https://forums.sharpcap.co.uk/viewtopic.php?p=7736)). They are useful only as a cross-check. One processing test is worth doing. Integrate the lights without registering them, and run PixInsight's LinearDefectDetection on the result ([PixInsight LDD-LPS tutorial](https://pixinsight.com/tutorials/LDD-LPS/)). Any bands that survive there are fixed to sensor rows, and a master row template removes them. Bands that do not survive in that integration but do survive in the registered stack are residual errors from the levelling itself, most likely bright stars biasing the row estimates. The best correction would subtract each row's optical-black (masked) pixel average, which no star can contaminate ([US7564489B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7564489)). Nothing found shows that the 183C's SDK can read those pixels out, so that is a question for Touptek.

## Ask the Altair 183 Facebook groups first, then Touptek on GitHub

The live discussion of Altair 183C banding happens in the **Altair Astro Facebook group** and the **"ZWO/QHY/Altair Astro 183 users group"**. Cloudy Nights and Stargazers Lounge threads on this camera are few and mostly unanswered ([Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/6673441896094108/); [Cloudy Nights](https://www.cloudynights.com/forums/topic/589537-altair-astro-hypercam-183c-and-sharpcap/)). The best first post is a reply under the existing 183C banding thread, addressed to the owner with the dew heater, asking whether it still works and whether that camera also dropped long frames in the cold. For the long-exposure failure and the optical-black question, open an **indilib/indi-3rdparty GitHub issue**. Touptek's engineers commit SDK updates to that repo, and the INDI maintainer tags @touptek on Touptek driver issues ([indi-3rdparty #1395](https://github.com/indilib/indi-3rdparty/issues/1395); [indi-toupbase history](https://github.com/indilib/indi-3rdparty/commits/master/indi-toupbase)). For a hardware verdict or a firmware file, raise an **Altair support ticket** at altairastro.help. Altair asks for a Diagnose File and a camera test from AltairCapture, which needs Windows, so the Windows PC can produce both ([Diagnose File](https://www.altairastro.help/info-instructions/troubleshooting/how-to-generate-a-diagnose-file-in-altaircapture/); [camera test](https://www.altairastro.help/info-instructions/troubleshooting/how-to-run-a-camera-test-for-altair-technical-support/)). Altair's support has been called slow ([Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/3188080564630276/)), so start it early and run it in parallel. Robin Glover answers banding questions himself on the **SharpCap forum's Altair section** ([SharpCap forum](https://forums.sharpcap.co.uk/viewtopic.php?t=5068)).

Whichever venue, the question should include:

- the camera (Hypercam 183C, 2017, non-Pro, fan-cooled, USB-powered), firmware 1.4.1, SDK 60.31589, and OS and INDI versions;
- gain, offset, speed level, bit depth, and RAW capture;
- the cable, port and hub, and that the laptop runs on mains;
- the ambient temperature and humidity of the bad nights against the indoor test;
- two or three raw single darks and lights from a bad night, plus a matching indoor set;
- the row-noise RMS for each Bayer channel compared with per-pixel read noise, and whether consecutive darks share bands;
- a stretched crop of one sub and of the stack, before and after levelling;
- the long-exposure failure, with the exact log lines;
- everything already tried: offset 0 and 50, fan on and off, dithering over 18 minutes, and levelling per row and per Bayer site in four sections.

Including the results of steps 1 to 4 turns a "has anyone seen this?" post into one that people can actually diagnose.

## Conclusion

The research changes the frame of the problem. This is not a mysterious sensor flaw awaiting a firmware cure. It is a USB-powered camera whose readout is sensitive to its supply, and in the cold that supply or its connections look marginal. That makes the long-exposure dropouts probably the same fault as the banding, not a separate SDK bug. A warm, firmly connected, cleanly powered camera should fix both together. A test where the camera is cold but the laptop is indoors would prove the shared cause, and nobody in the public record has run that test cleanly on an IMX183.

What stays uncertain is whether the cold acts on the camera or on its connections, and whether this unit is healthy. The dew-strap cure rests on one unverified report, and a slowly worsening pattern would point to a failing camera rather than a fixable path. `stacking.destripe` is already more refined than any off-the-shelf tool. A careful write-up of the indoor/outdoor dark comparison would itself be a contribution the Altair 183 community lacks.
