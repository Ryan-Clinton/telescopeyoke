# The camera's row stripes: what others have found

Researched 19 October 2026, for the green and magenta row stripes of the Altair Hypercam 183C (`docs/validation.md`, `stacking.destripe`).

There are three separate things here, and they should not be run together:

- **A. Ordinary IMX183 row banding.** The sensor in this camera is known for random horizontal readout banding, new in every frame, strongest at low gain and with little signal, and averaging down in a stack. QHY's manual for its own IMX183 camera puts it down to the ADC: "after heavy stretch you may see some random banding on the background. By stacking more image these banding will be gone since it is the random" ([QHY183C manual](https://www.qhyccd.com/file/repository/PDF/CameraManual/QHY183C_Manual_EN.pdf)). A test of IMX183 cameras across makes found the same, worst at low gain and much weaker as gain rises ([Cloudy Nights IMX183 test thread](https://www.cloudynights.com/forums/topic/599475-sony-imx183-mono-test-thread-asi-qhy-etc/page/2/)). Our camera has this, and it needs no fault to explain it.
- **B. The increase on 17 October.** The stripes measured 2.0 to 2.4 ADU a frame on the cold, damp night (about −1 °C) against 1.4 to 1.6 on every earlier night, about 40% more, and were nearly gone indoors and warm on 18 October. This is the real question.
- **C. Long exposures not arriving**, on the same cold night, under INDI and under Altair's own library alike. It may share a cause with B, but that is not shown.

**What is known about B and C so far.** A temperature-, moisture-, USB-connection- or power-sensitive problem is the leading explanation for the increase and for the failed frames, but nothing yet tells these apart, and a developing fault in the camera is not ruled out. The one report closest to ours is an owner of the same fan-cooled 183C whose banding starts below about 10 °C, gets worse as it gets colder, and stopped when a dew heater was wrapped round the camera ([Altair Astro Facebook group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/8796706147100995/)). That is one person's experience, unchecked. The next step is a controlled cap-on test as the camera cools, changing one thing at a time (below), and a support request to Altair with the figures.

## What we know of our own set-up

- **The lead.** The camera's own lead is gone. A new 3 m USB 3 lead was bought about 5 October 2026 and has been used since 6 October. It has been tried in three laptop ports: two on the right and one on the left.
- **The USB history** (the kernel log, which records each connection's port and speed): on 3 and 4 October the camera was linked at USB 2 (480 Mb/s, port 1-5) on its old lead; from 6 October at USB 3 (5000 Mb/s) on the new lead, on ports 2-4 (13 and 17 October), 2-5 (15 and 18 October) and 2-1 (18 October, indoors). The stripes were 1.4 to 1.6 ADU on the USB 2 night and on the USB 3 nights alike, and the good 13 October and the bad 17 October used the same port, 2-4. So neither the link speed nor the port explains the increase on its own. The 3 m lead is not ruled out: it is the one part that was outdoors in the cold on every night.
- **No disconnects.** On the cold night the camera never dropped off USB; frames simply stopped arriving.
- **Settings tried.** Fan on and off, black level 0 and 50: no difference. Readout Speed 0, 1 and 2 gave 4.9, 3.6 and 5.6 on the stripe score, one frame each, which is too few to choose by. Gain 1500 gave weaker stripes than 900 at both 16 and 32 s; 32 s gave weaker stripes than 16 s at both.

## What other owners report

Horizontal banding on Altair 183C and 183M cameras is widely reported, mostly in the Altair Astro Facebook group and the "ZWO/QHY/Altair Astro 183 users group". The cures owners report as having worked are mostly electrical: fastening a USB plug that had play in the camera's socket ([183 users group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/465061658206316/posts/1107541107291698/)); a powered USB hub where worn laptop ports were blamed; a new lead; moving a power cable away from the USB lead ([Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/6673441896094108/)); an unpowered USB 2 hub on a 183C Tec ([Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/permalink/1946139525491059/)); a different laptop port ([Cloudy Nights](https://www.cloudynights.com/forums/topic/822496-ngc-4725-challenge-techniques-and-progress-discussion/page/2/)); a separate hub in place of the one in a power box, for a QHY183M ([SGP forum](https://forum.sequencegeneratorpro.com/t/qhy183m-is-this-normal-or-do-i-have-a-problem/9633)). Hubs cut both ways: others found a hub made banding worse ([Cloudy Nights](https://www.cloudynights.com/forums/topic/861976-horizontal-banding-noise-created-by-usb-hubs/)).

An Altair 26C owner had banding outdoors that could not be made indoors, and Altair found the camera "working perfectly" ([SharpCap forum](https://forums.sharpcap.co.uk/viewtopic.php?p=34183)). At the other end, a 183C ProTec owner's banding "just got worse" whatever he changed until the sensor failed ([Altair Astro FB](https://www.facebook.com/groups/altairastro/posts/8796706147100995/)), and ZWO sent an ASI183MM Pro back for repair after cables, supply, settings and laptop had all been swapped ([ZWO forum](https://bbs.zwoastro.com/d/12355-asi183mm-pro-horizontal-lines)).

**Gain.** Owners in the 183 users' group say not to go below "unity" and name 400 as the sweet spot ([183 users group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/465061658206316/posts/1107541107291698/)). Altair's own current advice for the AA183C is gain 800, black level 50, 12-bit RAW and exposures of 15 s or more ([Altair help](https://www.altairastro.help/info-instructions/cmos/what-gain-settings-to-use-with-altair-hypercam-cmos-astronomy-cameras/)). Gain scales differ between software and driver versions, so neither number carries over as it stands. What both agree on is that low gain makes the banding worse, which is what we measured. The gain for this camera is chosen from our own measurements (`camera_test.py --gain-sweep`), judged in ratios, not ADU (below).

## How supply trouble could make it worse

In the engineering literature, random row noise comes from noise on a sensor's supply or ADC reference voltages, which shifts a whole row's level at once during readout ([arXiv 2012.03666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03666); [US7508430B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7508430)). SharpCap's author suspects "fluctuations in the power supply voltage (USB) when the image is being read from the sensor", and says different PCs regulate USB better or worse, though he has never proved it ([SharpCap forum](https://forums.sharpcap.co.uk/viewtopic.php?t=5068)). The fan-cooled Hypercam has no external supply: it is powered entirely from USB ([Altair fan vs TEC FAQ](https://www.altairastro.help/info-instructions/faq/what-are-the-advantages-or-disadvantages-of-altair-hypercam-fan-cooled-cameras-vs-tec/)). Its own regulators and decoupling stand between the USB 5 V and the readout, so USB noise does not reach the sensor directly; but trouble on the USB supply or connection can disturb those internal rails, and so remains a plausible cause of an increase in readout banding.

Two more things fit B and C without any supply trouble: damp air at −1 °C condensing inside a body the fan draws outside air through, and a marginal contact (a worn socket, a loose plug, a joint) that changes as metal contracts. One Altair 183C Pro owner on INDI had timeouts that cleared after 10 minutes to an hour, "as if it needs to warm up", and in the same thread Altair replaced a faulty camera ([INDI forum, Altair 269c](https://www.indilib.org/forum/ccds-dslrs/10249-altair-269c.html)).

**The colour.** If the R/G rows and the G/B rows of the RGGB mosaic take slightly different offsets, or an offset changes along a row, debayering turns it into green or magenta, and one side of the frame can differ from the other. Patents on row correction note that treating all colours alike "is wrong for CFA sensors" ([US7508430B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7508430)), which is why `stacking.destripe` levels each Bayer site in sections across the frame.

## Dithering and longer frames

Dithering spreads out patterns fixed in the sensor; it does nothing for a row offset that is new in every readout ([Stargazers Lounge](https://stargazerslounge.com/profile/46703-wimvb/content/page/32)). That matches our own test: planted stars were found to G 17.38 ± 0.02 dithered and 17.36 ± 0.02 not, over 18 minutes. Dithering is kept all the same, for hot pixels, dust and imperfect flats; it is just not the cure for this.

Longer frames are the practical help. The row noise comes once a readout, so a 32 s frame gathers twice the sky of a 16 s one for the same one disturbance, and our measurements agree (weaker stripes at 32 s). M33's patrol takes 32 s frames. Whether 32 s is better overall is decided by depth found with planted stars (`depth.py --plant`), not by the stripe score alone, and only while stars stay round, little is burnt out, the frames keep arriving and the exposure timing holds.

## Firmware and SDK

No firmware list, changelog or download for the Hypercam 183C exists in public, and no maker has published a fix for IMX183 row banding. Altair's library can flash firmware from Linux, but its own header warns a power loss during the update leaves the camera only fit to "be returned to the factory for repair" ([toupcam.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/indilib/indi-3rdparty/master/libtoupcam/toupcam.h)), and INDI users who flashed newer firmware were left with cameras "completely defunct or... partially functional" ([indi-3rdparty #1114](https://github.com/indilib/indi-3rdparty/issues/1114)). **Firmware 1.4.1 is not a known cause; nothing is flashed without Altair's own file and instructions.**

**What the camera says about itself** (Altair's Diagnose File, made in AltairCapture on the Windows PC, 19 October 2026; `./camera_test.py --capabilities` reads the same from the laptop through the same library): made 7 June 2017, hardware 1.0, firmware 1.4.1.20170111, FPGA 1.1, revision 1, a fan, and **no frame buffer**. Without one, each row goes down the lead as it is read, so anything that holds the USB link up during readout meets the frame itself, where a camera with a buffer would simply send the frame late. That fits USB and supply trouble showing as stripes and lost frames, though it proves nothing on its own. On the Windows PC the camera came up as USB 2.0; on the laptop it has connected at USB 3 (SuperSpeed) every time since 13 October.

INDI now carries Altair's library 60.32226 (August 2026) ([altaircam.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/indilib/indi-3rdparty/master/libaltaircam/altaircam.h)), newer than the 60.31589 on Altair's own download page and in use here ([Altair downloads](https://www.altairastro.help/downloads/)). One INDI user found 57.27348 the last version with no timeouts ([#1114](https://github.com/indilib/indi-3rdparty/issues/1114)). Changing the library is the last test, not the first (below). The long-exposure failure happened under Altair's library without INDI too, which clears INDI but not the library, the USB stack, the laptop's USB controller, the camera or the cold.

**Warming indoors, 19 October 2026.** The camera came in after a night on the telescope (a food probe on its body read about 15 °C), spent a while on the Windows PC for the Diagnose File, then went on the laptop's USB 3 port 2-1 on the 3 m lead, cap on, face down under a cloth: `camera_test.py --cooldown`, 16 s darks, 20 a set, from 18:14 to 19:36 (`cache/cooldown-20261019.jsonl`).

| 18:28 | 1.34 | 0 | 16.6 | +0.58 ADU | 1205 ppm |

The 19:26 set's four lost frames are its last four: the camera was unplugged at 19:35:45 (the kernel's own disconnect) to make room for the focuser. Every one of the 116 frames taken with the camera connected arrived. The sensor was still warming, its darks climbing to a plateau, and the stripes stayed at the ordinary 1.34 to 1.40 ADU, if anything rising a little with the warmth, never near the cold night's 2.0 to 2.4. So a camera warming on the same lead, laptop and USB 3 link shows neither fault. What it did not test is cold or damp: it never began near −1 °C, and indoors the air is far from its dew point. That is test 3, outside.

## The tests, in order

One thing changes at a time; everything else (gain, exposure, readout speed, lead, port, library) stays as it was, or the result says nothing. Each condition gets 20 to 30 frames. Darks are the measure throughout: they need no sky, and no star upsets the row estimates.

| # | Test | What it decides |
|---|------|-----------------|
| 1 | **A control, indoors.** `./camera_test.py --cooldown --steps 1 --note "indoors, control"`, cap on, the same gain, exposure and speed used for everything after. | The camera's warm baseline on this lead and port, to compare everything with. |
| 2 | **The shortest good lead, straight into the laptop.** The camera's own lead is gone, so a short (1 m or less) USB 3 lead in place of the 3 m one, no hub or extension, indoors. It must be one that can be trusted: shielded, sold as USB 3 (SuperSpeed, 5 Gb/s), from a known maker, and seen to link at 5000 Mb/s in the cooldown record. A cheap lead that is itself marginal tells nothing. | Whether the 3 m lead adds anything when warm. |
| 3 | **Cooling outside, cap on.** `./camera_test.py --cooldown --note "3 m lead, port 2-4"`: 12 sets of 20 capped frames, every 10 minutes, as the night cools. Each set keeps the stripes (ADU, the score, and electrons once test 8b has run), failed frames and the time a frame takes, and with them everything that should not change, so nothing has to be remembered: the gain, the exposure (how long the shutter is open) and the longer time the driver is asked for to give it (from the trail test), the readout speed, fan and black level, the USB port and speed, the other USB devices plugged in (the mount's lead, the focuser), whether the laptop is on its charger, the outdoor temperature and dew point and the margin between them, and the camera's own temperature where its library gives one (this camera has no thermometer, so it is blank). In its place the darks themselves: their mean over their median and the share of hot pixels, which rise and fall with the sensor's temperature (dark current roughly doubles every 6 °C). | Whether the stripes and failures grow as it cools, and whether they follow the temperature or the dew margin. If they come on as the margin falls below a degree or two, whatever the temperature, that points to damp rather than cold electronics. Whether the stripes and the failed frames follow the same thing, or are two faults. |
| 4 | **Warm only the camera.** The same, with a dew strap round the camera body on its own battery, not the mount's supply, and the fan and vents left clear. | If the stripes fall while the air stays cold, the trouble is thermal or damp in the camera or its socket. It does not show that cold electronics are the cause: a heater also keeps damp off, warms the plug and socket and changes the fan's air. |
| 5 | **The lead and contact**, at the same temperature: the plug tied firmly to the camera body; the short lead in place of the 3 m one; the other laptop port. | Whether a loose contact or the lead is to blame. |
| 6 | **A powered hub**, at the same temperature. Not assumed to help: a hub's own regulator can add noise. | Whether the laptop's USB supply is the weak point. |
| 7 | **The readout speeds**, 20 to 30 frames each (`--gain-sweep --gains 1500 --speeds 0 1 2 --frames 20`). | Whether one speed beats the rest, now with enough frames to tell. |
| 8 | **The gains**, judged on the stripes against the sky's grain and on what is found, not on ADU (`--gain-sweep --frames 20`). | The gain for this camera. |
| 8b | **Electrons per ADU at each gain**, cap off, pointed at an evenly lit field (a light panel, or a white cloth over the tube in daylight): `./camera_test.py --electrons --gains 900 1500 2000`. From pairs of frames at five exposures, half the variance of each pair's difference against its mean (photon transfer). Kept, so the sweep and the cooldown then give the stripes in electrons. | The stripes in electrons, comparable across gains. |
| 9 | **The laptop's power saving**, for the failed frames: USB autosuspend off for the camera, then deep CPU idle states limited ([Altair help](https://www.altairastro.help/info-instructions/troubleshooting/interrupted-frames-subs-download-during-imaging-session/)). | Whether the laptop is cutting the camera off mid-frame. |
| 10 | **The library**: 60.31589 against 60.32226, and 57.27348 if needed. | Whether the library is behind the failed frames. |
| 11 | **Altair**, with the results, if the trouble stays tied to temperature whatever the lead, port, laptop and library. | A repair, or a known cure. |

A fridge would separate the camera's temperature from the outdoor electrics, but it brings damp, slow and uneven cooling and a different USB route, and the camera has to stay sealed until it is back above the dew point. Test 3 outdoors with the cap on answers most of the same question safely, and comes first.

**Judging a gain sweep.** Stripes in ADU cannot be compared across gains: a higher gain gives more ADU for the same electrons, so ADU noise can rise as the true read noise falls. `camera_test.py --gain-sweep` gives the stripes four ways: the raw ADU, the ADU over one pixel's sky grain (`stripes_per_grain`), the score against what the pixels' noise would give a row (1 is none), and electrons (`stripes_e`) once `--electrons` has measured that gain. It is judged on the ratios and electrons and on stars found and pixels burnt, never on ADU.

**What would point to a fault in the camera.** The stripes still climbing week after week with the same lead, laptop, port, library, gain, speed, exposure and temperature; or, reliably, severe stripes and failures when the camera is cold whatever the computer and USB route, and none when it is warm in the same set-ups. If a heater cures it every time, it still goes to Altair: a camera that only behaves above about 10 °C is not right for astronomy.

## Processing

**Checking the correction does not take real light.** Any row correction can take some of a galaxy with the stripes, if its estimate of a row's level includes the galaxy, and that would bias faint fluxes, which matters for discovery work. `./depth.py SESSION --destripe-check` plants stars of known brightness in a session's raw frames, before anything else is done to them, and measures each frame with and without `stacking.destripe`: the share of planted stars found, their measured flux over what was put in, and how much the broad light changed. Since a galaxy is broad light, not points, it also plants a faint galaxy of known profile in each frame (an exponential disc of random size, shape and turn, its centre one to five times a pixel's noise) and measures what the correction kept of it in elliptical rings: its central surface brightness, its profile out to four scale lengths, and its total.

It found a real fault on 19 October 2026. Each row's level was the median of every pixel in the row, so it followed a galaxy narrower than the 101-row window, and levelling it took real light: in synthetic frames a galaxy 280 rows across lost 12% of its core and one 140 rows across 38%. Now each row's level is measured from its sky alone. A first levelling finds where the galaxy or nebula is, and the second leaves that light out. The same synthetic galaxies lose about 2% and 4%. On real frames (5 frames each, 1500 stars planted):

| Session | Found, plain | Found, destriped | Flux kept, plain | Flux kept, destriped | Broad light changed | Galaxy centre kept | Galaxy total kept | Galaxy at 3-4 scale lengths |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M31, 17 October | 89.3% | 90.4% | 0.998 | 0.992 | 1.8% | 1.000 | 0.992 | 0.981 |
| M81, 19 October | 89.3% | 91.9% | 0.998 | 0.993 | 6.8% (7.1% the old way) | 1.000 | 0.995 | 0.996 |
| M33, 19 October | 89.9% | 90.7% | 0.998 | 0.992 | 3.6% | 0.998 | 0.996 | 0.993 |

The correction finds 0.8 to 2.6 points more of the planted stars and keeps 99.2% to 99.3% of their light, against 99.8% without it: a bias of under 1%, small beside the scatter of the photometry, but there, and to be watched. "Broad light changed" counts the stripes taken out too, so it is not all galaxy. M31, M81 and M33 are large against the window, which is why the old way had done little harm to these sessions. The fault mattered for smaller galaxies.

The planted galaxies keep their central surface brightness to 0.2% and 99.2% to 99.6% of their total light; what is lost is in the outskirts, 1% to 2% at three to four scale lengths. The synthetic test (`tests/test_stacking.py`) finds the limit: a galaxy whose centre is only a pixel's noise bright keeps 90% of its light and 78% at four scale lengths, because its outskirts are below what the light finder sees and pull their rows' levels up. Widening the found light by four blocks brought that to 97%, but left more of a stripe that changes along its row near the frame's edges (0.30 to 0.43 ADU), and made no clear difference on the real frames, so it was not kept. The test holds each case where it is now.

Siril's `seqfixbanding` and SharpCap's Banding Suppression both level whole rows from row averages, as `stacking.destripe` does ([Siril docs](https://siril.readthedocs.io/en/latest/processing/banding.html); [SharpCap forum](https://forums.sharpcap.co.uk/viewtopic.php?p=7736)), and are useful only as a cross-check. One test is worth doing: integrate the lights without registering them and run PixInsight's LinearDefectDetection on the result ([PixInsight LDD-LPS tutorial](https://pixinsight.com/tutorials/LDD-LPS/)). Bands that survive there are fixed to sensor rows, for a master row template to take out. The best correction would subtract each row's masked (optical-black) pixels, which no star can upset ([US7564489B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7564489)); nothing found shows the 183C's library can read those out, which is a question for Touptek.
