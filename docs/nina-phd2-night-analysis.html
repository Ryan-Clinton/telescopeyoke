Questions it answers

  • PHD2 said it was guiding, so why are the frames trailed?
  • Why did NINA stop taking frames for twenty minutes?
  • Was it cloud, focus or guiding that spoiled the second half?
  • How many usable minutes did the night give, and where did the rest go?

What it does

companion.py reads the frames your capture program saved (FITS or XISF), PHD2's guide log (or PHD2 live, through its server) and, if you give it, NINA's own log. Every frame is measured: stars, sharpness (FWHM), roundness, sky brightness, sensor temperature against its target, and the guiding during it. Each is judged against the best frames of the same target and filter that night, so no figure from another telescope is assumed. Frames are then called dimmed, bright sky, soft, trailed, off cooling target or guide star lost, and stretches with no frames are explained from PHD2 and NINA where they can be. What was measured is always said apart from what is only inferred.

It never touches the observatory. It has no command that can move a mount, take a frame, turn a focuser or a wheel, or change a setting.

Running it

On Windows, with Python 3.11 or newer, from the development version (Code > Download ZIP on GitHub, then install.ps1; no config.toml needed):

python companion.py check "C:\path\to\tonights\frames"
python companion.py report "C:\path\to\tonights\frames" --phd2-log "...\PHD2_GuideLog_DATE.txt" --nina-log "...\NINA\Logs\DATE.log"

watch instead of report follows a night as it happens. The full guide, every option and how to read a report: The companion.
