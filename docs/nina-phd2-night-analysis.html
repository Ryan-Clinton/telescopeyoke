Why did last night go wrong?

A read-only companion for an observatory that already works: NINA, SGP, APT, SharpCap or Ekos taking the frames, PHD2 guiding.

How to run itPost what it said about your night

Questions it answers

  • PHD2 said it was guiding, so why are the frames trailed?
  • Why did NINA stop taking frames for twenty minutes?
  • Was it cloud, focus or guiding that spoiled the second half?
  • How many usable minutes did the night give, and where did the rest go?

What it does

companion.py reads the frames your capture program saved, PHD2's guide log (or PHD2 live, through its server) and, if you give it, NINA's own log. Every light frame it can read is measured (FITS, or XISF uncompressed or zlib-compressed); what it cannot read or leaves out (calibration frames, stacks, colour pictures, other compression) is listed, never silently dropped. For each: stars, sharpness (FWHM), roundness, sky brightness, sensor temperature against its target, and the guiding during it. Each is judged against the best frames of the same target and filter that night, so no figure from another telescope is assumed. Frames are then called dimmed, bright sky, soft, trailed, off cooling target or guide star lost, and stretches with no frames are explained from PHD2 and NINA where they can be. What was measured is always said apart from what is only inferred.

It never touches the observatory. It has no command that can move a mount, take a frame, turn a focuser or a wheel, or change a setting.

No images to hand? Start with the logs

The NINA and PHD2 logs alone are enough for a first look, a few megabytes instead of a night's frames:

python companion.py report --nina-log "...\NINA\Logs\DATE.log" --phd2-log "...\PHD2_GuideLog_DATE.txt" --night 2026-09-13

Here is what it said, in full, about a night that another observatory published: NINA 3.2 running a sequence on M 27 with PHD2 guiding and a dome (their logs, 13-14 September 2026), read offline:

From the logs alone: no frame was measured here; any judgement of the frames is NINA's own star detection, where its log has it.

From the logs alone it told 27 ten-minute lights from 62 autofocus, plate-solving and focusing exposures, found that 7 of the 27 had gaps in their guiding, and put more than two hours down to the sequence waiting. What it could not say without the frames is how sharp each light really was: for that it used NINA's own star counts, and said so. The same observatory's November night, and others' NINA and PHD2 logs, are in What has been tried.

Running it

On Windows, with Python 3.11 or newer, from the development version (Code > Download ZIP on GitHub, then install.ps1; no config.toml needed):

python companion.py check "C:\path\to\tonights\frames"
python companion.py report "C:\path\to\tonights\frames" --phd2-log "...\PHD2_GuideLog_DATE.txt" --nina-log "...\NINA\Logs\DATE.log"

watch instead of report follows a night as it happens. The full guide, every option and how to read a report: The companion.

How far it is proven. Not in the 0.4.0 download: it is on the development branch, added on 19 November 2026. Tried on the author's own frames, on made-up nights with known faults, and offline on NINA and PHD2 logs other observatories have published. No other observatory's night with its frames, logs and guiding together has been read yet, and it has not yet run live beside anyone else's observatory.
