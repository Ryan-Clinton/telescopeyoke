# Replacing the EQ3's up-down (latitude) bolts

The two bolts that tilt the polar axis up and down on the EQ3 Pro are
broken, so altitude can only be set to about half a degree. This page is
what forums and the Sky-Watcher parts trade say about replacing them,
gathered on 16 November 2026. None of it has been tried on this mount yet.
The forum threads are about the EQ3-2. The EQ3 Pro is the same head with
motors fitted, so the latitude parts should be the same, but check yours as
you go.

## This mount's bolt

**Straightened, 16 November 2026.** During Gary's service of the mount that
evening the south bolt was straightened and turns again, so the head did not
have to come off for it. The steps below are kept for a bolt that bends
again; the new 12.9 bolts (ordered below) are still the lasting fix.


The broken bolt is the south one, at the polar-scope end, so the long one.
The user thinks it is the bolt that raises the axis. It won't turn in or out.
It is probably bent, but nobody has
checked. The other bolt still lowers the axis; raising it needs a hand on
the head.

Because this bolt won't turn either way, the easy routes are out:

- It can't simply be unscrewed.
- The forum trick of screwing it in a few turns and sawing off the bent end
  needs a bolt that still turns inward.

So the head has to come off its fork:

1. Take the tube, the counterweights and the motors' leads off.
2. Get the glued covers off (below).
3. Hold the head and take out the pivot bolt and its lock nut. Note where
   the two plastic spacers go.

With the head off, the bolt carries no load. That shows which case it is:

- **It turns now.** It was only jammed under the tab. Unscrew it.
- **It still won't turn, and the bend is outside the casting.** Saw it off
  above the casting, then unscrew the straight stub.
- **It still won't turn, and the bend is inside the thread.** It needs
  drilling out and the thread re-tapping, M8. That is a job for someone
  with a drill press, or a reason to ask Gary.

**Ordered 16 November 2026:** two M8 x 80 mm grade 12.9 cap head bolts,
threaded all the way up
([eBay 236709921725](https://www.ebay.co.uk/itm/236709921725),
£7.80 delivered), due 19 to 25 November. They turn with a 6 mm Allen key.

## The parts

- **Thread: M8.** Teleskop-Express sells Sky-Watcher's own spare
  (part SKE35, €26) as an "M8 screw" for the EQ3 and CG4. They also say it
  fits other mounts with an M8 female thread at the latitude adjustment
  (EQ2, CG3, older CG4, EQ5).
- **The two bolts are different lengths.** The long one goes on the
  polar-finder side. The lengths sellers give don't agree: 48 mm short and
  78 or 83 mm long (365Astronomy, quoted on Stargazers Lounge), and 40 mm
  for the EQ3-2 (Grover's Optics). Measure the old ones before buying.
  One forum poster thinks theirs bent because the short bolt had been put
  where the long one goes.
- **Steel, not stainless.** One poster says to use a hard steel bolt, not
  stainless, because stainless "will bind with aluminium" (galling).
  Another says ordinary stainless metric bolts from a hardware shop did
  the job. Plain high-tensile steel (grade 8.8) with a smear of grease
  avoids the argument. A hardware-shop M8 bolt with a nut locked on as a
  handle, or a printed knob, works as well as the spare part.

### Bolts that won't bend (16 November 2026)

- **What decides bending is the steel's yield strength.** A grade 12.9
  socket cap bolt yields at about 1080 MPa. Stainless A2-70 yields at about
  450 MPa, and the soft stock bolts are lower still.
- **Stainless isn't the upgrade it sounds like.** A Stargazers Lounge member
  fitted Astro Developments' stainless bolts to an EQ5, and the long one
  bent.
- **Best choice: M8 grade 12.9 socket cap bolts**, at the measured lengths,
  with a press-on or printed knob on the head. Grease the thread to stop
  galling.
- **They must be fully threaded.** Long socket cap bolts are usually
  threaded for only 28 mm, which isn't enough travel.
- **Two packs found on Amazon UK (16 November 2026):**
  - [PATIKIL M8 x 80, 12.9, full thread, 10 pieces, £9.39](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0F1XZTLTP),
    for the long bolt.
  - [PATIKIL M8 x 50, 12.9, full thread, 25 pieces, £13.99](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0F1XK96V1),
    for the short bolt.
  - Check both lengths against the good bolt first.
- **Like for like:** Grover's Optics in Northallerton has the Sky-Watcher
  bolt for the EQ3 Pro, long or short, at £20 to £30. Material not stated.

### Caps

No shop sells the side covers as a spare; only the polar-scope cap is
listed. If one breaks:

- Ask Sky-Watcher's UK distributor or First Light Optics' spares desk for
  the part.
- Or measure it and print one, or fit a black push-in blanking cap of the
  same diameter.

The covers only keep dirt off the pivot bolt.

## When the covers don't need to come off

On this mount they do (above). In general, though, they usually don't. A latitude bolt just screws through the mount's casting and
pushes on the tab (the "paddle") under the head. To swap one that is
straight enough to turn:

1. Take the tube and counterweights off, or at least take the weight off
   the bolts by holding the counterweight bar.
2. Back the other bolt off.
3. Unscrew the old bolt all the way out, put in the new one with a little
   grease, and set the altitude roughly by eye.

You only need the covers off when a bolt is **bent** or has **gone under
the paddle** and is jammed. Then the head has to come off its fork to free
it. That is the case the two posts below are about.

## The glued covers: heat gun or prise?

Both posts are on Stargazers Lounge, "Latitude bolt stuck" (September
2018), about an EQ3-2 with a badly bent latitude bolt:

- What's there: there is a black plastic cover on each side of the head,
  held on with **hot glue**. Under each one is the pivot bolt and its lock
  nut. The pivot bolt also screws into the fork, and there are two plastic
  spacers between the fork and the head. Take off the nut and the bolt, and
  the head lifts off.
- One poster says to use a heat gun, kept moving "so the plastic shields
  don't burn or deform".
- The other says that even moderate heat from a heat gun spoiled the shiny
  finish of their EQ3's covers, so they just prised the covers off with a
  knife. Another poster on the same forum, about an EQ3-2, said theirs came
  off with a fingernail under the rim.

So both work. The heat gun risks the look of the covers. Prising risks
cracking them: the covers are "somewhat fragile", says a poster in another
thread. Neither risks the mount itself. The covers are only for looks, so a
cracked one still does its job.

### A hot water bottle?

A hot water bottle is safe and worth a try, but it probably won't do much
on its own.

- Hot glue goes soft well above body heat. A hot water bottle is filled
  hot, not boiling. The heat then has to get through the rubber and the
  plastic cover. Meanwhile the aluminium head behind the glue draws the heat
  away. The glue will get warm, but probably not soft.
- It cannot spoil the finish, which is the heat gun's problem.
- It helps more with the tube and counterweights off and the head indoors
  at room temperature. A head straight out of the November cold will take
  a long time to warm.

What would likely work better, gentlest first:

1. **A hairdryer** on hot, held close and kept moving for a couple of
   minutes. It gets hotter than a hot water bottle and is much gentler than
   a heat gun. Try the rim the moment you stop.
2. **Isopropyl alcohol** run into the seam with a cotton bud. Hot glue lets
   go of smooth surfaces when alcohol gets under it. This is a DIY-audio
   forum report, not a telescope one, so test it on a hidden spot first.
3. **Prise with something plastic rather than a knife.** Use a guitar pick,
   an old bank card or a phone-repair spudger. Work it in at the rim, and
   lift a little at a time at several points round the edge rather than
   levering hard at one. The glue has let go when the cover moves with
   little force. Stop and warm it again if it creaks.

## A bent bolt

- **Don't force a bent bolt out.** It can tear the thread out of the
  aluminium casting.
- Instead, screw it **in** a few turns so the bent part comes out past the
  casting. Saw the bent end off, then unscrew the straight part that is
  left. A poster on Cloudy Nights cut a stuck Atlas bolt out in two places
  with a Dremel.
- If the thread in the casting feels rough afterwards, clean it with an M8
  tap before putting a new bolt in. A rough thread will chew up the new
  bolt.

## Keeping the new ones straight

The bolts are a push-pull pair. They bend because one bolt is used to lift
the whole head, tube and counterweights against the other:

- Before turning one bolt, back the other off.
- When raising the axis, lift the counterweight bar a little by hand so the
  bolt isn't carrying the load.
- Don't do them up hard. Snug is enough: they won't turn by themselves.

On this mount, turning the rear bolt in lowers the axis, by about 2.2° a
turn. Raising it means turning the front bolt in (measured 13 October
2026).
