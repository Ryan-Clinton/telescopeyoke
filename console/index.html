Welcome to TelescopeYoke

This is what is ready and what still needs doing before a night at the telescope.

Camera

"Set up" checks the camera is plugged in and has a driver, takes Altair's library files out of their SDK if they are missing, and takes a test frame.

Plate solver

    
ASTAP works out where the telescope is really pointing from the stars in a frame. It needs its program and the D20 star database.

    

  


  

    
Webcam

    
A webcam pointed at the telescope photographs it during every move, so you can look before agreeing to one.

    

    

  


  

    
Polar alignment

    
Measures how far the mount's polar axis is from the pole, by plate solving at three positions, and says which way to move the mount. Start with the telescope tracking a target well away from the pole: use Go to with centring first.

    

    

      
      

        

        

        

      

    

    
Before dark, from Polaris alone

    
Polaris shows in a short exposure by day, when the plate solver has nothing to go by. Get the focus near first (Focus, on the most distant thing in view): a star out of focus will not show against a bright sky. Then see whether the sky is good enough, find the star from the home position, and measure. The measurement stays on the star and says how far there is still to go while you turn the bolts.

    

      
      
      
    

    
  


  

    
Azimuth by landmark

    
With the polar axis's height set once, all that changes from one setting-up to the next is which way the mount faces. Remember a distant fixed thing (a chimney, a mast, a street lamp) on a night when the star measurement says the axis is right. Next time, by day, Check turns the telescope back to it and shows where it sits now; turn the azimuth bolts until it is on the cross. Put the tripod on its marks and set home the same way each time.

From a phone panorama

    
The quick way: no motors, a few minutes. Stand where the telescope stands, hold the phone at the height of the tube, tilted up so the sky is above everything, and take a panorama of the skyline. Copy it to this computer.

Rigs

    
A rig is one telescope, with its own settings and its own pictures. With two telescopes out, each runs in a TelescopeYoke window of its own, and this shows what both are doing. Nothing here asks a mount or a camera anything: it reads what each rig last wrote.

    

    
To add one, in a terminal: ./app.py --new-rig NAME (on Windows, python app.py --new-rig NAME). It makes the rig's settings and puts "TelescopeYoke (NAME)" in the applications menu. The motion lock stops every rig at once.

  


  

    
Doctor

    

    

    

      
      
    

    
A hardware report says what this computer is, what the mount says it is, and every check above, with your location left out. Nothing is moved to make it. Posting one, working or not, is how other mounts and cameras get onto the list of what has been tried: github.com/Ryan-Clinton/telescopeyoke

About

    

    
TelescopeYoke plans the night, slews a SynScan mount, plate-solves, helps you focus and stacks short exposures. Every move is checked against its limits and shown as a plan before anything turns.
